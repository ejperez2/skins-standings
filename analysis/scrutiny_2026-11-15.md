# Is the Chance to Win too kind to the leader? (written 2026-11-15)

Question: the page gives Eristeo 70% (Brian 17%, Adam 13%) after ~13 games per team. Early favourites lost in
2022-23 and 2023-24. Is 70% inflated?

**Answer: no. On every test below the 70% is fair or slightly low. My best estimate is about 75%, plausible range
65-85%. The floor under deliberately pessimistic assumptions is about 53%.** The model has two real weaknesses, but
both currently lean against the leader, and one will start to matter from about January.

All earlier scrutiny (`win_prob_backtest_2026-11-07.md`) rested on 4 seasons, so 4 winners. This round adds
**24 seasons** (2000-2026, 82-game seasons only): 715 preseason win totals from Basketball-Reference and all 29,000
games, every team-season checked against its final record (`archive/history/preseason_win_totals_2000-26.csv`,
`games_2000-26.csv`). Code and results: `analysis/scrutiny_2026-11-15/`.

## Where the 70% comes from

| | Eristeo | Brian | Adam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Projected at the draft | 506 | 484 | 479 |
| Skins above opening pace so far | +10.8 | +1.6 | +3.0 |
| Change in projection for games left | +20.4 | −2.0 | +5.0 |
| Projected now | 537 | 484 | 487 |

The lead is 50-53 projected skins. In the simulation the gap to each rival has an SD of 56-66 skins, so each gap is
about 0.8-0.9 SD. Three things could make 70% too high: the gap is overstated, the SD is too small, or the errors
are shaped differently from what the model assumes. Each is tested below.

## 1. Is the hot start over-weighted? No.

Across 24 seasons, the best forecast of a team's remaining win rate after 13 games puts **27% weight on the record
so far** (95% interval 22-33%) and the rest on the preseason line.

This season the weighted average moved 27.2% of the way. Split into parts, it matches the historical optimum almost
exactly:

| Weight on | History's best (19 seasons with scores) | Weighted average this season |
|---|---|---|
| Pull of the opening line toward .500 | −0.089 | −0.087 |
| Record so far | 0.105 | 0.079 |
| Point margin so far | 0.192 | 0.214 |

By source: Vegas 22%, CBS 26%, ESPN 30%, TeamRankings 35%. In 2023-26 the Vegas line under-reacted early (12-21%
at 10-17 games), so there is no history of these sources chasing hot starts.

Consequence: the "ignore hot start" toggle (58%) is a lower bound, not a neutral view. It assumes 13 games carry no
information, which 24 seasons reject.

## 2. Is the gap itself overstated? By a few skins at most.

An independent projection built only from opening lines, records and point margins with the 19-season coefficients
gives Eristeo 529, Brian 488, Adam 483: a lead of 41-46 instead of 50-53. All 13 projections available today
(4 core sources, 5 trial sources, 4 formula variants) put Eristeo ahead by at least 39.

Two findings argue for shaving the gap slightly:
- **Preseason lines are about 10% too extreme.** Final wins regress on the line with slope 0.90 (season-bootstrap
  95% interval 0.84-0.95). Teams lined at 25 wins or fewer beat the line by 3.0 wins on average (se 1.1). Eristeo
  holds the most extreme roster, so this costs him about 3 skins more than his rivals. Today's projections have
  already applied that pull (first row of the table above).
- In leagues shaped like this one (section 4), the leader's gap shrank by 4-6 skins on average from this point
  (se 3-5, so not clearly different from zero).

Deep L picks are not a trap later on: teams projected under 27 wins after 13 games finished 0.5 wins *below*
projection on average, and 1.0 below from 30 games (late-season tanking helps L picks).

## 3. Is the team-level spread too small? No, it is too wide now and too narrow later.

- 2025-26, the one season `TALENT_SD` 8.6 was fit on, was the fourth-worst year for preseason lines in 24. The
  24-season value is **7.2** (season-to-season SD about 1.6; RMS preseason miss 8.4 wins).
- Tails are normal: 715 misses, excess kurtosis 0.09, 6 beyond 2.5 SD against 8.9 expected.
- Remaining projection error by games played (history formula, held-out seasons) against the model's curve:

| Games played | 5 | 13 | 20 | 30 | 41 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| History | 6.6 | 5.5 | 4.9 | 4.1 | 3.3 | 2.5 | 1.8 |
| Model, 8.6 × (left/82)^1.5 | 7.8 | 6.6 | 5.7 | 4.3 | 3.0 | 2.1 | 1.2 |

The model is about 20% too wide now and 35% too narrow by game 60. The exponent 1.5 assumes a team's quality is
fixed; in reality it drifts (injuries, trades, tanking), so uncertainty about the remaining games fades more
slowly. This matches the earlier finding that favourites at 30-50 games underperformed. The archived real sources
beat the formula from about 35 games (RMS miss 4.1 vs 4.7 wins), which closes roughly half of the late gap.

## 4. Is the player-level spread too small? No. This is the main result.

Player-level spread is driven by the roster correlation (0.25): it triples the variance of a 10-team roster. Its
value came from 4 seasons.

**League transplant.** I put this league's exact shape onto each of the 19 thirty-team seasons since 2005 at the
same point of the season: whoever owns today's k-th best projected team, on the same W/L side, owns that season's
k-th best. Teams were projected with the history formula fit on the other seasons, the live model was run
(schedule simulation, 0.25, in-season refit), and the result was compared with what happened. Twelve more leagues
per season came from nudging projections by ±2 wins before ranking.

| Games per team | Leader slot: model said | Leader slot: actually won | Seasons won, exact transplant |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 56% | 67% | 12 of 19 |
| **13 (now)** | **61%** | **78%** (95% interval 70-85%) | **17 of 19** |
| 20 | 66% | 85% | 17 of 19 |
| 30 | 75% | 89% | 17 of 19 |
| 41 | 84% | 96% | 19 of 19 |
| 50 | 90% | 98% | 18 of 19 |

At 13 games, over all three slots (741 forecasts): forecasts of 16% won 7%, 24% won 16%, 59% won 76%, 69% won 87%.
The gap between two rosters missed by an SD of 29-34 skins against the model's 55-64. The 80% player ranges held
99% of outcomes. Applying each past season's miss to today's gaps keeps Eristeo first in 87% (95% interval 82-91%).

**Why not simply believe 87%?** Transplanted rosters are dealt by rank, and their teams miss together about 0.00.
Real drafters cluster. Re-estimated on the 2022-26 real rosters with a permutation test:

| Misses measured from | Same-roster correlation | Rough 95% interval | Chance a random re-deal matches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preseason line | 0.08 | −0.03 to 0.18 | 26% |
| Game 13 | 0.18 | 0.07 to 0.28 | 0.6% |
| Game 20 | 0.08 | −0.03 to 0.19 | 15% |
| Game 41 | 0.18 | 0.07 to 0.29 | 0.2% |

So clustering among real drafters is real, about 0.08-0.18, and 0.25 sits at the top of the interval. This season
so far it is 0.00 (interval ±0.13). The truth for this league lies between the transplant (0.00, 87%) and the
model (0.25, 70%).

## 6. Why earlier favourites lost

They barely led. Projected lead over second place in mid-November, against a typical player miss from that point
of 19 skins (5-6 team rosters):

| Season | Leader | Lead over 2nd | Finished |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022-23 | Thomas | 4 | 5th |
| 2023-24 | Matt | 7 | 2nd |
| 2024-25 | Brian | 14 | 1st |
| 2025-26 | Adam | 6 | 1st |
| 2026-27 | Eristeo | 50 | ? |

Those leads were 0.2-0.7 of one typical miss, with four or five rivals each. This lead is 0.8-0.9 SD over each of
two rivals even on the model's wide spread, and about 1.5 SD on the spread history supports.

## What can still go wrong

- 70-75% means losing more than one time in four. A 50-skin gap is about 50 results out of the roughly 1,380 games the two
  rosters still have to play.
- Brian is the bigger threat (17% vs 13%): his results move opposite to Eristeo's (correlation −0.16).
- The model has no injuries or trades. A long absence on DET, LAL or CLE, or a surprise run by UTA or CHA, moves
  several skins at once.
- The transplant uses a formula, not the real sources, and rank-dealt rosters. It bounds the answer; it does not
  replace the model.

## Recommended model changes (not made; the live page is untouched)

1. **Cap the in-season `TALENT_SD` refit.** It reaches 100% weight at 41 games, but across 24 seasons the refit
   value had correlation 0.3 with later misses at 13-30 games and −0.2 at 41. It ranges 0-10.5 at 13 games. A cap
   near 20-30% weight, centred on 7.2, fits the evidence. This matters most: a quiet first half would otherwise
   make the model sharply over-confident in January.
2. **Replace the (left/82)^1.5 fade** with a curve that follows the table in section 3 (wider from about 35 games).
3. **Roster correlation.** 0.25 is cautious. 0.15 is the centre of the evidence. Keeping 0.25 is a defensible
   choice if the league prefers to under-state the leader, and it currently offsets item 2.
4. **Relabel the "ignore hot start" toggle** as a pessimistic bound.

Items 1 and 2 should go in together before about game 30. Changing only item 3 would raise the leader's number
without fixing the late-season weakness.

files from the downloaded Basketball-Reference pages, the FiveThirtyEight game archive and ESPN's scoreboard.
