## Evidence tanking is getting better
- Bottom-8 teams are giving young players fewer competitive minutes (32%, against 40–44% at this point before). That fits teams trying to win close games.
  - **Caveat:** this year's bottom 8 include veteran rosters (Clippers, Bucks), so this may be roster make-up rather than a choice.
- The worst 3 are slightly better than usual against their rating (−3.1 vs −4.3 on average), well within normal swings.

That's thin. Nothing clearly points to improvement.

## Evidence it's getting worse
- **Against the moneylines:** bad teams are losing more than priced.
  - Bottom 8 won 30.6% against 36.2% implied, which is −6.9 wins, and were below the market in every week.
  - Favourites in L-vs-L games are +10 pts against the market.
  - This is the strongest signal, but there are no per-game moneylines from past seasons to compare with.
- Ranks 4–10 are running 3–4 pts below their rating, against about 0 at this point in past seasons. Under the old flat bottom-3 rule, this zone tanked late in the season.
- The worst-3 teams have the lowest closing-lineup share in three years. That's the opposite of "fighting to escape the bottom 3", but it's only 8 games.
- Each of these is roughly one standard deviation from normal.

## Evidence of no change
- The loss-size pattern matches last season almost exactly: same final margin, and the same share added after the game is decided.
- How young minutes shift from competitive to decided games is the same as 2025-26.
- Regulars sitting is flat across zones and in line with past seasons.
- The blowout spike is league-wide, part of a decade-long rise, and bad teams' share of it is unchanged (1.4×).
- Favourites beat their preseason lines by the same amount as last season at this point.

## Overall
No clear change yet, with a slight lean toward "not better". The lean rests mostly on market numbers that have no past baseline.

More importantly, these tests can't really detect it yet. In every past season, tanking showed up after the trade deadline:
- Under the old flat bottom-3 rule, ranks 4–10 ran −4 to −5 against their rating over the last 25 games, but about 0 in November.
- Regulars missing in the tanking zone jumped to about 24% after Feb 1.

The tests that matter come in February–April:
- whether the worst 3 stay at or above their rating, when they used to sit around 0 in the flat-odds era;
- whether shutdowns move from ranks 4–10 toward play-in seeds 9–11;
- how the daily lottery-odds snapshots line up with results.
