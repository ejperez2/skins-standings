How skinsy is each season?

League-wide skins %: the share of all team-games in which the drafted outcome happened (W picks winning, L picks losing). Rebuilt game by game from every result, 2022-23 to 2026-27 (2026-27 through Nov 4: 121 games).

  • It barely moves the finish. The 121 games played are a tenth of the season. With them banked and the rest going as the preseason lines expect, the projected final is about (80% range ). That fits the models' 58–61%.
  • Both sides of the formula ran hot. The league is unusually lopsided this early, and the draft is catching more of that than usual. When a W pick plays an L pick, the W pick has won 76% of the time, against 64–70% at this point in the last four seasons.
  • The deadline doesn't make the skins game skinsier. Post-deadline skins % was below the game 121-to-deadline rate in 3 of the last 4 seasons. Tanking only adds skins when the tanking teams were drafted as L picks.

How skinsy each season is, at a glance

Every team-game either produces a skin or it doesn't. Skins come from two kinds of games:

  • Games between two W picks or two L picks always give exactly one skin out of two, so they hold every season near 50%.
  • W-pick vs L-pick games give two skins or none. They are where seasons pull apart.

Is 63.6% just luck?

This is the same simulation, frozen at 121 games. Every earlier season was between 58% and 60% at this point, so this season stands out. Still, about 1 simulated season in 12 is at least this skinsy with no change in how teams behave.

Early numbers have been a fair guide in past seasons. After 121 games, each of the last four seasons was within about 2 points of its final. This season's early figure is 4–5 points above its projected final. That's more than past seasons have moved, but most of the excess will wash out as the other 90% of games are played.

Why it's high: a lopsided league, drafted well

Skins % has two ingredients:

  • How lopsided the league is. This is measured as the best possible skins %: every team counted on its better side (wins if it's above .500, losses if below). It needs no draft, so it can be compared across all eight seasons.
  • How much of that the draft captures. Actual skins % falls short of the best possible whenever a team was drafted on the wrong side.

This season has a lot of both. The best possible after 121 games is , the highest of the eight seasons (2018-19 was close at 69.8%). And the draft has captured about two-thirds of the edge over 50%, against 53–64% at this point in earlier seasons.

Mechanically, a game between two W picks or two L picks always gives one skin each way, so only W-pick-vs-L-pick games move skins % away from 50%. About half of games are W-vs-L in every season. What changes is how often the W side wins them:

The draft also sets how many W-vs-L games there are. An even split of W and L picks makes the most of them. This season's 15–15 split is the most even yet (earlier drafts had 17–21 W picks), so slightly more games are W vs L. That accounts for only a small part of the excess. At last season's 18–12 split, this season's 76% W-pick win rate would give 63.0% instead of 63.6%.

Was 2022-23 the least skinsy because of bad drafting?

No. 2022-23's draft caught 83% of the edge that was available, second only to 2023-24. The league itself was flat: with every team counted on its better side, the best possible skins % was only 59.1%, the lowest of all eight seasons. Too many teams finished near .500 for any draft to score much.

The weakest drafting was in the last two full seasons (66–67% of the edge). Those leagues were lopsided enough to cover for it.

The drafts nearly always follow the preseason betting lines: only one or two teams a season go against them. So following the lines exactly would have given almost the same skins % every year, and "drafting quality" mostly means how right the lines turned out. Does the trade deadline make it skinsier?

Tanking after the deadline makes the league more lopsided (see the tanking report). But it only adds skins when the teams doing the losing were drafted as L picks. A W pick that collapses after the deadline costs its owner skins.

In practice, post-deadline skins % was lower than in the game 121-to-deadline stretch in 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25, and only 0.9 points higher in 2025-26.

Method: skins rebuilt from ESPN game results with each season's drafted W/L picks (archive rosters for 2022-26; the 2026-27 draft in index.qmd). Rebuilt finals: 57.6%, 61.6%, 58.6%, 59.3%. The NBA Cup final, which ESPN lists as a regular-season game but which doesn't count in the standings, is removed.

Simulation: each team's strength comes from its Oct 20, 2026 consensus win total, converted with (wins − 41) ÷ 2.7. Each simulated season also redraws every team's strength with a 3.2-point standard deviation, the typical preseason miss (8.6 wins, from this repo's model). Home edge (1.75 points) and per-game spread (14.0 points) are fit on 2025-26. Games are played on this season's actual schedule, 4,000 times. Undetermined NBA Cup games are left out (1,200 of 1,230 games).
